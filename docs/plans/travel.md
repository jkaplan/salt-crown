# Plan: The march

Status: **agreed, steps 1-4 of 6 implemented** — 2026-09-06

Went agreed → proposed → agreed in one day. Desk research
([research/travel-games.md](../research/travel-games.md)) invalidated the first
version's central mechanic hours after it was agreed, so it went back to
proposed rather than being quietly patched; the chase, and then the decision
that the chase gates an *ending* rather than survival, is what earned the
status back. What changed and why is in "What the research overturned" below.

Stage-4 note: the riskiest assumption — that a march makes a run *better*
rather than merely *longer* — is still unspiked. The research sharpened it
into something more specific and more testable, which is progress, but the
first playable version is still the test.

## Why this exists

The run should feel like Oregon Trail: the road is a place things happen, not
a number paid once. Today `march()` is instant — camp ends, Water is charged,
battle starts.

The stronger argument, unchanged: **travel adds almost no verbs. It gives the
ones already in the game a reason to exist.** Treating a wound, benching a
body, running a machine are all near-automatic today.

## What the research overturned

Three corrections, kept on the record because the reasoning is the value.

**1. Pace was backwards, and had nothing to push against.** The first draft had
Forced and Killing *saving* Water by spending fewer days. Oregon Trail charges
food **per mile, rising with pace** — grueling 4/mile against normal's 2 — so
pushing costs roughly double for the same distance. What it buys there is
calendar days, because winter is coming. Salt Crown had no calendar, so the
mechanic was both inverted and unmotivated.

**2. "Leaving units behind" was the wrong problem.** Battle Brothers charges
full upkeep for reserves exactly as Salt Crown does, so that is not an anomaly
to fix. The reason to bench anyone, in every game that makes it matter, is that
they are *recovering* and recovery is *rate-limited*. Salt Crown heals free and
automatically, so nothing forces rotation at all.

**3. A leg that is only flavour plus a drain is the known failure mode.** Banner
Saga players describe waiting dozens of days where nothing changes "only your
caravan dies out for the missing supplies."

## The fix: you are chasing something

The quarry is **N days ahead**. It moves at its own rate. Every march, your pace
against its pace moves N.

**The gap is the clock, so no calendar is needed.** Pressure is generated by the
chase itself rather than bolted on beside it, and every march gets a legible
outcome beyond arriving: a number that moved the right way or the wrong way.

### The chase gates an ENDING, not survival

Losing the trail is deliberately **not** a fail state:

- **Catch the quarry** (N reaches zero) — the true ending.
- **Lose the trail** (N passes its limit) — the run *continues*. You finish it
  as a warband fighting, salvaging and upgrading, and reach a lesser ending.
- **Lose a battle** — the run is over, exactly as today. Unchanged.

This is the single most load-bearing decision in the plan, and it earns its
place three times over:

1. **It makes pace a real question.** Not "do I survive" but *"do I burn my
   warband for the better ending?"* Spend supply and bodies to hold the gap
   closed, or play safe and settle for less.
2. **It resolves the run-structure fork below.** Because the chase gates an
   ending rather than *being* the structure, the 15-encounter/3-act shape is
   untouched and every completion baseline in `ROADMAP.md` stays meaningful.
3. **It dodges the trap in the alternative.** "You can keep playing but you
   cannot win" is miserable. There is still a win here — just a smaller one.

**Stated condition, recorded rather than glossed:** the continue-on branch is
only worth having *if the game is fun enough to sustain it*. If the lesser
ending is a chore, the honest fix is to make losing the trail end the run
after all. That is a judgement to make after playing, not before.

It also answers something the game currently fudges — why this warband keeps
marching into worse fights. Because it is after something.

**The fiction is deliberately open.** What is being chased has not been chosen,
and nothing below depends on it.

## What changes

### 1. Three paces — Column, Forced, Killing

Now correctly oriented: **pushing costs MORE supply, and buys ground.**

| Pace | Water | The gap | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Column** | baseline | quarry gains | — |
| **Forced** | more | holds even | morale; they arrive worn |
| **Killing** | most | you gain | worse morale, **and bodies die** |

Names are real marching terms and need no tooltip. Pace **locks** for the
march; a trail event may offer to change it as one of its branches, which
costs no new UI because `events.ts` already renders branching choices.

### 2. Every leg carries a decision or a change

Directly from finding 3. A leg may not be flavour text plus a drain. Each one
resolves *something*: an event with a real branch, a wound worsening, ground
gained or lost, a machine failing. If a leg has nothing to say, there should be
fewer legs.

### 3. Death on the road is the wound ladder, not a new roll

**Fodder only** — never a named Prospect or Officer. A hard pace **escalates a
wound rung**, so the body that does not get up is *the one that was already
hurt*. Nothing dies at random; it dies of something visible in camp beforehand
that `TREAT_COST` could have prevented.

### 4. Straggling, keyed to discipline

Historically the dominant cost of hard marching, and a *discipline* problem
before a fatigue one — which maps onto a stat the game already has. Mutants
(0.35) straggle badly; True Kin (0.78) hold; Machines (1.0) never straggle,
they break. Stragglers rejoin a leg or two later, or they don't, making
*arriving early* versus *arriving whole* a real tension.

### 5. Recovery becomes rate-limited — the rotation prerequisite

From finding 2. Wounds stop healing free and automatically. Recovery needs a
benched slot, takes real time, and competes for salvage. This makes the
existing fielding cap of 6 bite, makes benching mean something, and makes "who
sits out this march" a question every camp.

Listed here because travel is what gives it teeth, but it is separable and may
well ship first — it improves the game with or without a road.

### 6. Legs, distance, and the other three factors

Legs fixed at **3** for the first version. **Distance is visible at camp before
the buy** — the condition that makes variable march length safe later, because
a hidden variable distance is an unearned run-killer while a visible one is a
forecast you plan around.

Plus: machines wear on the road (reuses `applyCondition`); camp warns which
bodies are likely to worsen at the pace being considered; and a trail event can
trade salvage for Water, the same trade Scavenge already makes.

## Deliberately not in this

**The fence moved once, deliberately:** an earlier draft ruled out any death
outside the wound ladder. Killing pace kills. Allowed through on two conditions
— fodder only, and routed *through* the wound ladder rather than around it.

Still out: **no food system** (agreed in shape, below, not built here); **no
baggage-train upgrades** (same); **no map, route choice, or branching paths**;
**no terrain or weather**; **no load or weight stat**; **no battle-time effect**
— travel changes what *arrives*, never how the fight works.

## How it gets proved

Baseline is the current post-Instability reading: **9.0-10.0% greedy completion
over 300 runs**.

```

                     draft predicted lower spend, which was the error.)
```

## Open questions

- ~~**Run structure.**~~ **Resolved** by the ending-gate decision above: the
  chase threads through the existing 15-encounter/3-act structure rather than
  replacing it, because it gates which ending you reach rather than how long
  the run is. Nothing in the difficulty curve or the harness baselines has to
  move.
- ~~**What "losing the trail" does.**~~ **Resolved**: the run continues to a
  lesser ending. See above, including the condition attached to it.
- **Two numbers, or one?** Distance-to-next-encounter and gap-to-quarry are
  both distances shown at camp. That may be one number too many for a screen
  that has to stay readable. Possibly they are the same number wearing two
  labels. Does not block the data model — only the screen.
- **Exact numbers** for pace costs, quarry speed, and the gap's limit. Pick
  against `sim:campaign`, never by feel.
- **What is being chased.** Open on purpose.
- **Alternative objectives and quests along the way** — raised alongside the
  ending-gate decision: *"maybe there are alternative objectives from the start
  or quests along the way."* Genuinely new scope, and a natural extension of an
  endings system rather than part of it. Noted so it is not re-derived; **not
  in this plan.** Worth revisiting once there are two endings to sit between.

## Agreed but not built: two systems this one sets up

### Food — scales with days, where Water scales with bodies

Food only earns a slot if it is not a second Water. **Water is charged per
body; Food is charged per day on the road.** A big warband on a short hop is a
Water problem; a small warband on a long haul is a Food problem.

Note for whoever builds it: until Food exists, pace moves **Water**. When Food
arrives it takes over the days-based axis and Water should revert to pure
headcount. Battle Brothers runs ~2 provisions per man per day, more on hard
terrain, with shortfall causing morale loss then desertion — a useful reference
point, not a number to copy.

### The baggage train — a camp that travels

Not a place. Upgrading a camp you abandon every march makes no sense; what gets
upgraded is the column's kit — rain catchers on the wagons, a medic's tent, a
salvage-sorting wagon. Historically right: armies carried their camp. Every
upgrade costs supply to haul and slows the column, so each is a trade.

Stated goal, in the player's own words: *"other ways to feel closer to your
warband and to help them."*

Loop Hero is the closest reference and also the warning — its camp is genuine
meta-progression across runs, and its most common criticism is that the
endgame becomes grind. A per-run train avoids that shape.

## Step 4 reading — the chase, and four tuning passes

The endings are wired. Getting there took one structural correction and four
measured tunings, and the structural one is the finding.

**Catching must not end the run.** The first version ended it on the spot,
which made the chase gate SURVIVAL as well as the ending — the exact thing
the section above says it must not do. A shortcut is worth however much
campaign it skips, so whichever pace closed fastest simply won:

| gap / closing rate | Killing completion | Column completion |
|---|---|---|
| closes in 6 marches | 26.3% | 10.3% |
| closes in 4 marches | 63.3% | 10.3% |
| closes in 14 marches | 0.7% | 10.3% |

Widening the gap did not produce a middle, it produced an unreachable ending —
the attrition curve goes from survivable to fatal across a narrow band of
marches. **There is no number that fixes it**, which is what said the model
was wrong rather than the constants. The quarry is now run down at the END of
the road: every pace survives the same fifteen encounters, and the gap only
decides which ending is standing there.

**Forced was strictly dominated and had to start closing.** Holding the gap
exactly is worth nothing when closing it later is unaffordable — a bridge to
nowhere. It cost more water than Column and caught nothing. It closes slowly
now, which makes it the sustainable chase and Killing the sprint.

Where it landed, 200 runs per policy:

| pace | completion | caught | lost the trail |
|---|---|---|---|
| Column | 10.5% | 0% | 69.5% |
| chase (adaptive) | 6.5% | 2.5% | 1.0% |
| Forced | 3.5% | 3.5% | 0% |
| Killing | 2.0% | 2.0% | 0% |

No pace dominates, mixing beats committing, and the trade is the one this
plan asked for: **give up roughly seven points of win rate to convert a win
into the true ending.** Column is the only pace that reliably survives, and it
is the only one that can never catch anything.

**Not yet trustworthy.** Every number above is a placeholder reached by four
rounds of guess-and-check against one greedy bot on one origin. The adaptive
policy is deliberately crude and is the harness, not a player. Nobody has
played a single march of this.
